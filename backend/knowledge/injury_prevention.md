# Common running injuries and prevention

## How common are running injuries?
Systematic reviews report that roughly 20-80% of recreational runners sustain an overuse injury in a given year, with the knee, shin, foot, and Achilles most often affected. Most are overuse problems caused by loading tissue faster than it adapts.

## What are the main risk factors?
The most consistent factor is a previous injury. Others include rapid increases in volume or intensity, insufficient recovery, inadequate sleep, and sudden changes in shoes or surfaces. Evidence for any single biomechanical cause is weak.

## What is runner's knee (patellofemoral pain)?
Pain around or behind the kneecap, often worse on stairs, downhill running, or after sitting. Common contributors include rapid training increases and weak hip or thigh muscles. Reducing load and strengthening the hip and quadriceps usually helps.

## What are shin splints (medial tibial stress syndrome)?
Aching along the inner shin, usually early in a run and often after a jump in volume or switching to harder surfaces. It is a bone-stress reaction continuum, so ignoring it can progress to a stress fracture.

## How do I recognize a possible stress fracture?
Focal, pinpoint bone pain that worsens with impact, persists at rest or at night, or hurts to press on warrants prompt medical assessment. Continuing to run through it risks a complete fracture.

## What about Achilles and plantar problems?
Achilles tendon pain and heel or arch pain (plantar fasciitis) are overuse conditions that often respond to load management and calf and foot strengthening. Morning stiffness that eases as you move is typical.

## Is it pain or discomfort?
General muscle fatigue is normal. Sharp pain, pain that changes your stride, swelling, pain that worsens as you run, or pain at rest are reasons to stop.

## When should I see a professional?
Seek medical or physiotherapy advice for pain lasting more than about a week despite reduced load, any suspected stress fracture, swelling, numbness, or pain that disturbs sleep. The coach can describe general patterns but cannot diagnose injuries.
