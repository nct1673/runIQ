# Training load, progression, and recovery

## What is progressive overload?
Fitness improves when the body is stressed slightly beyond what it is used to and then given time to adapt. Training load is increased gradually: weekly distance, long-run length, or intensity rises a little at a time, with periodic easier weeks.

## Is the 10% rule reliable?
The "increase weekly mileage by no more than 10%" rule is a popular simple guideline, but evidence for it is mixed: some studies find no clear injury benefit from following it strictly. It is better treated as a rough cap for runners with a recent injury or a low base, not a law. Rapid spikes relative to your recent average appear riskier than any fixed percentage.

## What is acute versus chronic load?
Acute load is recent training, often the last 7 days; chronic load is the longer-term average, often 28 days. The ratio between them is used to describe how much a recent week deviates from what you are conditioned for. Research on this acute:chronic workload ratio linked sudden large spikes with higher injury risk, though the exact thresholds and the method itself are debated. Treat ratios as context, not a precise predictor.

## Why include easy weeks?
Many plans cut volume by about 20-30% every third or fourth week. These recovery weeks let tissues adapt and reduce accumulated fatigue, so the following block of training lands better.

## How many rest days do I need?
It depends on experience and load. Beginners benefit from two or three non-running days per week; experienced runners often take one. Rest or cross-training days are when adaptation occurs.

## What are signs of under-recovery?
Persistently elevated resting heart rate, heart rate higher than usual at the same pace, unusually slow paces on easy runs, poor sleep, low motivation, and lingering soreness are common signs of accumulated fatigue. A single bad day is normal; a trend over a week or more suggests backing off.

## How does sleep affect recovery?
Sleep is when much of the physical adaptation to training happens. Chronic short sleep is associated with worse performance, slower recovery, and higher injury risk. Most adults do best with seven to nine hours.
