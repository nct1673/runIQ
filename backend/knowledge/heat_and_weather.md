# Heat, humidity, and weather effects on running

## How does heat affect running performance?
Warm conditions slow runners because the body diverts blood to the skin for cooling, raising heart rate at a given pace and reducing the blood available to working muscles. Research on marathon results suggests performance starts degrading above roughly 10-15 degrees C, with slower runners affected over a wider range. Individual response varies a lot with fitness, acclimatization, and body size.

## Why does humidity make it harder?
Sweat cools you only when it evaporates. In high humidity, evaporation is limited, so body temperature rises faster for the same effort. Dew point is often a better guide than relative humidity: a dew point above about 15 degrees C feels noticeably oppressive and usually warrants slowing down.

## How should I adjust pace in the heat?
Run by effort or heart rate rather than holding your cool-weather pace. A common rule of thumb is to expect pace to slow by a few seconds per kilometre for each several degrees above your ideal temperature, but this is an estimate and varies per person. Slow down early, hydrate, and shift hard sessions to cooler hours when possible.

## What is heat acclimatization?
With 10-14 days of regular exposure to heat, the body adapts: sweating starts earlier, sweat is more dilute, plasma volume rises, and heart rate at a given pace falls. Gains fade within a few weeks without continued exposure.

## What are the warning signs of heat illness?
Dizziness, confusion, nausea, cessation of sweating, chills in the heat, or a sudden heart-rate spike are warning signs. Stop, cool down, and seek medical help if symptoms are severe. Heat illness can be dangerous and should not be treated as a pacing problem.

## How do cold and wind affect running?
Cold is generally less limiting than heat for performance, though very cold air can irritate airways and stiffen muscles, so warm up longer. A headwind costs more energy than a tailwind returns, so out-and-back runs in wind are usually slower overall than in calm conditions.
