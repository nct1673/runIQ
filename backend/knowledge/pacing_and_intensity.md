# Pacing and training intensity

## What is easy running and why does it matter?
Easy running is effort at which you can hold a full conversation. For most runners it sits roughly at 65-79% of maximum heart rate, or about 60-75% of heart rate reserve. Easy runs build aerobic base, strengthen tendons and bones, and allow recovery between harder sessions. A common mistake is running easy days too fast, which adds fatigue without a matching gain.

## What is the 80/20 model of intensity distribution?
Studies of endurance athletes, notably work by Stephen Seiler, found that many successful athletes do about 80% of their sessions at low intensity and about 20% at moderate-to-high intensity. For recreational runners this is a guideline, not a rule: it suggests most running should feel easy, with a small share of tempo, threshold, or interval work.

## What are the main intensity zones?
Zone models differ, but a typical five-zone scheme is: Zone 1 very easy recovery; Zone 2 easy aerobic; Zone 3 moderate or marathon effort; Zone 4 threshold or tempo (comfortably hard, sustainable for about 20-60 minutes); Zone 5 hard intervals near maximum aerobic capacity. Heart-rate zones depend on an accurate maximum or threshold heart rate, which formulas such as 220 minus age estimate poorly for individuals.

## What is cardiac drift?
During a steady run, heart rate often creeps upward even when pace is constant. This cardiac drift is driven by rising body temperature, dehydration, and fatigue. A large drift over an easy run can indicate heat stress, poor hydration, or accumulated fatigue. Comparing heart rate at the same pace across weeks is a common fitness indicator: lower heart rate at the same pace usually signals improving aerobic fitness.

## What is a tempo or threshold run?
A tempo run is sustained effort near lactate threshold, often 20-40 minutes at a pace you could hold for about an hour in a race. It feels comfortably hard. Tempo work improves the pace you can sustain before fatigue accumulates and is a staple of 10K to marathon training.

## How should interval sessions be paced?
Intervals are repeated hard efforts, typically 2-5 minutes near 5K effort, separated by jogging recoveries. The goal is consistent repeats: the last interval should be close to the first. Starting too fast and fading is a sign the pace was set too aggressively.
