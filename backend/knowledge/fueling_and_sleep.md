# Fueling, hydration, and sleep

## How much carbohydrate do runners need?
Daily carbohydrate needs scale with training load. Sports-nutrition guidelines suggest roughly 3-5 g per kg body weight on light days, rising to 6-10 g per kg on very heavy training days. Carbohydrate is the main fuel for moderate and hard running.

## Do I need to eat during a run?
Runs under about 60 minutes generally do not require fuel. For longer efforts, guidelines suggest about 30-60 g of carbohydrate per hour, with up to about 90 g per hour for events beyond 2.5 hours if the gut is trained to tolerate it. Practice in training, not on race day.

## How should I hydrate?
Drink to thirst for most runs rather than following a fixed volume. Over-drinking can cause hyponatremia, a dangerous dilution of blood sodium, especially in slower runners during long events. In hot conditions, replace sodium losses on long efforts.

## What should I eat before and after a run?
A carbohydrate-rich meal 2-4 hours before a long or hard run, or a light snack closer to the start, is typical. After hard sessions, carbohydrate plus about 20-30 g of protein within a couple of hours supports glycogen restoration and muscle repair.

## What is the difference between fuelling and weight loss?
Under-fuelling relative to training demands is associated with fatigue, poor recovery, injury, and hormonal disruption, a pattern called relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S). Weight-loss goals during heavy training should be approached carefully.

## How does sleep affect running?
Sleep restricts or supports adaptation: short or poor sleep is linked with worse performance, slower reaction, and higher injury risk. Seven to nine hours is the common target for adults, and heavier training can increase sleep need.
