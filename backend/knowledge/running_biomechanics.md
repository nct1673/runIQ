# Running form and biomechanics

## What is cadence and does a higher one help?
Cadence is steps per minute. There is no single ideal number; it rises naturally with speed and varies with leg length. Heiderscheit and colleagues found that raising cadence about 5-10% reduced loading at the knee and hip in a lab setting, so a small increase can help runners who overstride. Do not force a large jump: changes of more than about 10% can feel awkward and raise energy cost.

## What is overstriding?
Overstriding means landing with the foot well ahead of the body's centre of mass, often with a straight knee. It tends to produce a larger braking force at landing. It is commonly associated with low cadence. Shortening stride length slightly, or raising cadence, usually reduces how far ahead the foot lands.

## What is shank angle at touchdown?
Shank angle is the angle of the lower leg relative to vertical at the moment the foot hits the ground. A shank angled further forward at touchdown is a marker of overstriding, while a more vertical shank suggests the foot lands closer beneath the body. It is a useful single-number summary of landing position from side-on video.

## What is knee flexion and why does it matter?
Knee flexion at touchdown and through midstance describes how much the knee bends to absorb load. Some bend is normal and protective; a very straight knee at landing is linked to higher impact forces. Flexion also changes with speed, being larger at faster paces.

## What is trunk lean?
A slight forward lean from the ankles, rather than bending at the waist, is generally considered efficient. Excessive forward lean or a very upright, backward-leaning posture can change loading on the hips and lower back. A small lean of a few degrees is typical.

## What does arm swing contribute?
Arms counterbalance the rotation of the legs and pelvis. Efficient swing is relaxed, mostly forward and back, with elbows around 90 degrees. Crossing the midline or tensing the shoulders tends to waste energy.

## Does footstrike pattern (heel, midfoot, forefoot) matter?
Research finds no clear evidence that one footstrike pattern is best for all runners. Where the foot lands relative to the body (overstriding) matters more than which part of the foot touches first. Changing footstrike deliberately can shift load to the calf and Achilles.

## How reliable is video-based form analysis?
Angles measured from a single side-on video depend on camera position, frame rate, and pose-estimation accuracy, so they carry error of a few degrees. Compare your own measurements over time under similar conditions, rather than treating one number as a precise value.
